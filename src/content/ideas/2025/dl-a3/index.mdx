## Introduction

Sequence modeling is where the course makes the limitations of fixed-size summaries clear. Translation is not just classification over longer inputs. The model has to read one sequence, generate another, preserve meaning, handle order, and make coherent decisions one token at a time.

The assignment made that progression concrete by asking me to implement an LSTM, a seq2seq model with attention, and transformer-based variants. The main point was not only that newer architectures work better. It was that each architecture addresses a different bottleneck.

> [!note]
> I use the assignment only as context here. The goal of this post is to explain the concepts that matter, not to publish restricted implementation details.

## What the Project Asked at a High Level

At a high level, the project moved through three stages:

1. understand recurrence and gating well enough to build an LSTM,
2. move from encoder-decoder bottlenecks to attention,
3. compare recurrent models against transformer-style sequence processing.

That makes the assignment a compact map of modern NLP architecture history. The through-line is always the same question: how does the model preserve, retrieve, and use information from earlier in the sequence?

The repo work made that comparison more explicit than the prose alone does. It included separate training paths, saved best configurations, side-by-side model comparison scripts, and direct inspection of translated outputs under matched evaluation settings.

## Translation Is Conditional Sequence Modeling

A translation model does not predict one label. It predicts a target sequence conditioned on a source sequence:

$$
p_{\theta}(y \mid x)=\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_{\theta}(y_t \mid y_{<t},x).
$$

That factorization immediately creates several difficulties:

- the output length is not fixed,
- each new token depends on earlier output choices,
- and mistakes can compound over time.

These are not implementation details. They are the reason sequence modeling needs different architectures and evaluation methods than standard image classification.

## Seq2Seq Models Expose the Bottleneck Problem

Classic encoder-decoder models compress the source sequence into hidden states and then decode from that representation. The structure is straightforward: one network reads, another writes. The limitation appears once sentences get longer or more structurally complex.

The bottleneck framing predicts the failure modes you actually see:

- omitted content,
- repetitions,
- weak alignment between source and output,
- and degraded performance on longer sequences.

That is why the seq2seq stage of the assignment matters. It makes the architectural limitation feel inevitable rather than historical.

## Attention Turns Retrieval Into Part of the Computation

Attention is the idea that each decode step should be allowed to retrieve the most relevant source-side information dynamically instead of relying on one compressed context vector.

<Figure
  src={p3Fig2}
  alt="Attention map"
  caption="Attention can be understood as dynamic retrieval over token states, not just as another block in the network. Source: Lowyx GT DL Notes (CC BY 4.0), https://lowyx.com/posts/gt-dl-notes/."
  width={900}
/>

In its standard form,

$$
\mathrm{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V.
$$

The softmax weights determine which positions matter for the current decision. That simple move changes the whole character of the model. Alignment is no longer implicit and fragile; it becomes something the network can compute step by step.

This is the point where sequence modeling begins to feel more like information routing than memory compression.

**Illustrative toy snippet, not assignment code:**

```python
import torch

def scaled_dot_product_attention(q, k, v, mask=None):
    scores = (q @ k.transpose(-2, -1)) / (q.size(-1) ** 0.5)
    if mask is not None:
        scores = scores.masked_fill(~mask, float("-inf"))
    weights = scores.softmax(dim=-1)
    return weights @ v
```

The important part is the structure: compare keys against the current query, suppress invalid positions with a mask when needed, and turn those scores into a weighted retrieval step.

## Transformers Reorganize the Whole Computation

Transformers push the idea further by making attention, residual paths, layer normalization, and feed-forward blocks the dominant computation pattern.

<Figure
  src={p3Fig3}
  alt="Multi-head attention"
  caption="Multi-head attention matters because one relation type is rarely enough; the model benefits from attending in several subspaces at once. Source: Lowyx GT DL Notes (CC BY 4.0), https://lowyx.com/posts/gt-dl-notes/."
  width={900}
/>

The most important conceptual consequences are:

- recurrence is no longer the main path for combining information,
- many dependencies can be modeled in parallel during training,
- and positional information has to be injected explicitly because attention alone is permutation-invariant.

That last point is easy to miss. Attention gets the spotlight, but position encodings and residual structure are part of why transformer models can organize language at all.

The assignment made this clearer by forcing a comparison between recurrent and transformer-style models under roughly the same task. The newer model family was not just faster; it was built around a different answer to "where should relevant information come from at each step?"

## Decoding Is Part of the System, Not an Afterthought

Even with a trained sequence model, inference policy still matters. Greedy decoding, beam search, and sampling can produce materially different behavior from the same learned distribution.

<Figure
  src={p3Fig4}
  alt="Beam search"
  caption="Decoding policy is part of model behavior because it changes the search process used to turn probabilities into actual sequences. Source: Lowyx GT DL Notes (CC BY 4.0), https://lowyx.com/posts/gt-dl-notes/."
  width={900}
/>

That is why translation quality is really a model-plus-decoder property. If two systems are compared with different inference policies, it becomes much harder to tell whether the gain came from better learned representations or just a different search strategy.

One lesson from the project is that sequence evaluation is inseparable from inference design.

**Illustrative toy snippet, not assignment code:**

```python
# greedy decoding
tokens = [bos_id]
for _ in range(max_steps):
    next_id = model(tokens)[-1].argmax().item()
    tokens.append(next_id)

# beam search keeps several partial sequences alive
beams = [([bos_id], 0.0)]
for _ in range(max_steps):
    candidates = []
    for seq, score in beams:
        log_probs = model(seq)[-1].log_softmax(dim=-1)
        top_scores, top_ids = log_probs.topk(beam_width)
        for s, idx in zip(top_scores.tolist(), top_ids.tolist()):
            candidates.append((seq + [idx], score + s))
    beams = sorted(candidates, key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)[:beam_width]
```

This is a useful contrast because it makes decoding policy visible: one path commits immediately, while the other keeps several plausible continuations alive and scores them jointly.

## Metrics Need Error Analysis

Perplexity and BLEU are informative, but they compress too much. Translation errors are structurally different from one another. A model can get the broad meaning right while damaging word order, or preserve fluency while dropping a crucial content word.

<Figure
  src={p3Fig5}
  alt="Alignment and translation errors"
  caption="Alignment views are valuable because they make omission and reordering errors more visible than a scalar metric can. Source: Lowyx GT DL Notes (CC BY 4.0), https://lowyx.com/posts/gt-dl-notes/."
  width={900}
/>

What the assignment made concrete is that error taxonomies are more actionable than a single score. Once you separate lexical substitution, reordering, omission, and repetition, it becomes much easier to decide whether the next intervention should be architectural, optimization-related, or decoder-related.

The repo work reflected that too. There were separate scripts for reporting perplexity and comparing model outputs, because scalar metrics tell you that a difference exists, while direct output inspection starts to tell you what kind of difference it is.

## What This Assignment Made Concrete

The project turned several high-level NLP ideas into operational ones:

- **Bottlenecks are an architectural problem, not just a tuning problem.**
- **Attention is a retrieval mechanism, not just a named module.**
- **Transformers solve more than one problem at once: dependency modeling, training parallelism, and representational flexibility.**
- **Decoding and evaluation policies shape what "good performance" even means.**

This assignment is a compact setting for studying the historical arc from RNNs to attention to transformers. The motivation for that transition becomes much clearer once you work through the older bottlenecks directly.
